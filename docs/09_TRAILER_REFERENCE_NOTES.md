The trailer is a motion and pacing reference, not a recording of an implemented game. It conveys traveling with the shrine, defended sites, increasing pressure, a grove encounter, relay machinery and a final beacon. Its broad low-poly shapes and bright gray lighting are simpler than the still-art direction. Match the stills' teal/cobalt shadows, amber light, cream masonry, framing and silhouettes when they differ.

## Implementation decisions

1. The traveler always carries the same one physical core between camps. Deployment removes it from their back; packing removes it from the camp; delivery leaves the harness empty.
2. Real gameplay stays in a continuous 3D world. Exploration uses a free third-person camera; deployment raises it into a fixed angled defense view; retreat reverses this transition.
3. Shrines, towers and enemies must remain the same designs across camera changes. Generated morphing, popping, duplicated items, odd firing directions or impossible pathing are not intended mechanics.
4. The camera storyboard and game design documents take precedence over incidental AI video details.

## Trailer revision approach

Capture actual gameplay once the visual slice exists. Keep six beats: choose destination; deploy and defend; pressure rises; pack and escape; exploit a new terrain advantage; deliver restored sunrise. Preserve stable tactical framing during combat and use cinematic moves only when they communicate exploration or the ending.
